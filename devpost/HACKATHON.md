## Eligibility
Ages 13–21, any country Devpost supports. Solo or teams up to 6; every member must be able to explain their own work.

## What must be submitted
- Public GitHub repo with **multiple meaningful commits** — "the entire project uploaded in a single commit will be disqualified".
- README: project overview, technologies used, setup instructions, credits and external resources, **AI usage disclosure**.
- Project description on Devpost.
- Demo video, **3–5 minutes**, that explains what it does, the development process, the challenges, and what was learned (not only a product demo).
- Recommended: live deployment, screenshots, technology list, slides.

## AI policy (verbatim)
"AI tools are allowed and encouraged as learning resources. AI should assist your learning, debugging, brainstorming,
and development process—not replace your understanding or the majority of your work. Participants must understand and be
able to explain the code and decisions behind their projects. Significant AI usage must be disclosed in the README.
Participants must submit AI chat history/screenshots if requested by organizers."

Disqualifiers listed: pre-hackathon or plagiarized work, misrepresented contributions, single-commit history,
undisclosed external resources or AI assistance, code-of-conduct violations.

## How this differs from LexHack
- Judges are two generalists, not lawyers: delight, clarity and a visible learning story outrank rigor.
- Learning & Growth is the largest criterion. The video must narrate process, challenges and lessons, 3–5 min.
- The AI rule is stricter: majority of the work must be ours, code must be explainable, disclosure is mandatory,
  chat history may be requested. Keep `.claude/session-log.jsonl` (already produced per repo) exportable.
- Fresh repo and fresh code only: no lift from TritonPlan, WebReg, part107-course, 3D-Modeling-Campus, Axiom.
